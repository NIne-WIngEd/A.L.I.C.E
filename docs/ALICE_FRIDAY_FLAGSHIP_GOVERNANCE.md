# A.L.I.C.E.-Friday Flagship Capability Governance

**Version:** 1.0.0
**Status:** Owner-ratified and effective
**Decision owner:** MK Rayan
**Flagship product:** A.L.I.C.E.
**Default downstream product:** Friday
**Mandatory parity period:** Through at least completion of A.L.I.C.E. Phase 15

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **OWNER-RATIFIED FLAGSHIP CAPABILITY RULE:** A.L.I.C.E. is the flagship and mandatory default capability upstream. Through at least completion of A.L.I.C.E. Phase 15, Friday must receive every transferable A.L.I.C.E. capability. Friday may gain a new capability only after A.L.I.C.E. has implemented, evaluated, approved, and gained it, unless MK Rayan records an explicit exact-scope owner override.

## 1. Authority hierarchy

1. MK Rayan retains supreme product-family authority.
2. A.L.I.C.E. is the flagship, research authority, and default capability-precedent product.
3. Host-neutral contracts are generalized through the Personal Cognitive Kernel.
4. Friday is the downstream host-neutral consumer product.

Default capability flow:

```text
A.L.I.C.E. implements, evaluates, approves, and gains the capability
                              |
                              v
Stable identifier, evidence bundle, parity entry, and host-neutral contract
                              |
                              v
Friday eligibility, downstream productization, product evaluation, and release
```

Friday-first capability acquisition is prohibited by default.

## 2. Mandatory parity through Phase 15

Through at least completion of A.L.I.C.E. Phase 15:

- every transferable capability gained by A.L.I.C.E. enters Friday's parity backlog;
- temporary productization lag is allowed;
- permanent omission is not allowed;
- private memories, identity, credentials, owner-specific policies, adapters, weights, integrations, and private state never transfer;
- hardware, migration, privacy, support, and host-authority constraints may delay activation without removing destination parity.

Changing the parity commitment after Phase 15 requires an explicit owner-ratified decision.

## 3. Friday-originated capability proposals

When Friday identifies a capability A.L.I.C.E. does not have:

1. Friday seeks permission from A.L.I.C.E. and Rayan.
2. Friday submits the proposal upstream.
3. A.L.I.C.E. researches, implements or integrates, evaluates, and decides.
4. A.L.I.C.E. must approve and gain the capability itself.
5. The capability receives a stable identifier and evidence bundle.
6. Host-neutral portions are generalized.
7. Friday becomes eligible for downstream implementation and testing.
8. Friday passes its own isolation, privacy, migration, support, and product evaluations.
9. A.L.I.C.E. audits the exact Friday candidate.
10. Rayan approves the exact Friday candidate.

A proposal, experiment, or prototype does not establish Friday eligibility.

## 4. Before formal Friday handover

Until a qualified Friday maintenance team exists and Rayan records formal handover:

- Rayan directly governs Friday;
- Friday does not independently govern its capability roadmap;
- Friday work is limited to repository foundation, maintenance, and downstream productization of capabilities already eligible through A.L.I.C.E.;
- Friday may not independently implement, activate, test as a Friday capability, release, or ship a capability lacking A.L.I.C.E. precedent.

## 5. After formal Friday handover

After formal handover, the Friday team may independently manage maintenance, incident response, packaging, distribution, compatibility, hardware support, accessibility, product experience, downstream implementation of eligible capabilities, roadmap planning, and capability proposals.

Every new capability still requires permission from both A.L.I.C.E. and Rayan.

A Friday-discovered capability that A.L.I.C.E. lacks must still be proposed upstream first. Friday remains ineligible until A.L.I.C.E. approves and gains it.

Independent maintenance does not create independent capability sovereignty.

## 6. Owner override

Rayan may override any product-family policy and explicitly authorize Friday to gain a capability that A.L.I.C.E. does not yet possess.

An override must be explicit, exact-scope bound, artifact-bound where applicable, accompanied by a reason and risk disposition, recorded, and non-precedential unless the record explicitly states otherwise.

Requirements not explicitly waived remain active.

## 7. Production governance

The default production path requires verified A.L.I.C.E. capability precedent, an exact-artifact A.L.I.C.E. audit attestation, Rayan's exact-candidate approval, migration and rollback evidence, and parity-ledger disposition.

Emergency rollback, disablement, quarantine, and containment may occur without prior approval only when they add no capability or broader authority.

## 8. Phase 6.5 meaning

The Friday repository already exists. A.L.I.C.E. Phase 6.5 is therefore an independent-product readiness and certification gate, not the repository-creation event.

Until Phase 6.5 passes, Friday remains foundation-only and may not activate production runtime capability or real host state.

## 9. Supersession

This standard supersedes conflicting language permitting Friday-first capability implementation, testing, activation, release, or shipment.

Earlier language allowing a future Friday team to work independently applies only to maintenance, productization, or capabilities already eligible through A.L.I.C.E., unless an explicit Rayan override states otherwise.
