# Consumer Personal-AI Product Vision

**Status:** Ratified product direction
**Internal codename:** Friday
**Public product name:** To be selected separately
**Assistant name:** Chosen locally by each host
**Relationship to A.L.I.C.E.:** A consumer distribution of the same evolving personal-cognitive architecture. A.L.I.C.E. remains Rayan's owner-specific flagship and frontier research deployment.

## 1. Product thesis

The product internally codenamed **Friday** is not one assistant identity distributed to everyone. It is software that creates a distinct host-owned personal intelligence.

During onboarding, the host chooses the assistant's name, voice, identity settings, and initial operating relationship. The internal codename is never required to become the assistant's displayed name. One installation may become "Nova," another "Atlas," another "Maya," and another any name the host chooses.

Each installation begins with common software and model packs, but its host instance develops its own:

- encrypted experience ledger;
- inspectable memories, beliefs, and causal models;
- preference and decision models;
- procedural skills and generated software;
- source-trust and uncertainty models;
- user-specific retrieval and knowledge indexes;
- locally trained rankers, routers, adapters, and future weights;
- voice, interaction style, judgment, and self-model;
- portable encrypted Identity Capsule.

The resulting installation is not merely a generic chatbot with a custom prompt. It is a host-owned personal model stack whose behavior, learned state, skills, and eventually neural parameters diverge from every other installation.

## 2. Full capability-parity principle

Every capability ratified for A.L.I.C.E. is also part of the consumer product's destination architecture unless it is intrinsically owner-specific data rather than a transferable capability.

This includes:

- continuous selective learning;
- automated memory formation and reflection;
- world, causal, user, social, and self models;
- independent judgment and a persistent voice;
- multimodal perception;
- proactive planning and curiosity;
- universal tool and computer use;
- autonomous coding and procedural skill creation;
- scientific discovery and formal reasoning;
- self-evaluation and self-improvement;
- local model training and host-specific weights;
- operating-environment and physical-device integration;
- agent coordination and future frontier research.

A.L.I.C.E. may receive experimental capabilities first. The consumer product may trail while the feature is generalized, made hardware-aware, packaged, migrated, and supported. That lag is a delivery state—not a permanent capability ceiling.

## 3. Non-negotiable product promises

1. **The host names the intelligence.** The displayed assistant name and identity are local host state, not fixed product branding.
2. **Raw host data remains local by default.** Core use must not require uploading personal content to the vendor.
3. **No vendor-readable personal state.** Developer infrastructure must not possess the keys required to decrypt host memory, training data, adapters, or identity state.
4. **Personalization is deeper than retrieval.** The system may learn through memory, beliefs, skills, preference models, adapters, and future host-specific weights.
5. **Personalization is inspectable.** The host can view what the system knows, believes, predicts, and has trained from.
6. **Personalization is correctable and portable.** The host can edit, delete, export, migrate, or rebuild learned state.
7. **The assistant has judgment, not blind mimicry.** It develops a stable voice and may disagree while remaining subordinate to the host's constitutional authority.
8. **Model independence.** Host identity survives replacement of the underlying base model.
9. **Offline competence.** Core conversation, retrieval, learning, and common actions work without a network connection.
10. **Visible network behavior.** Every outbound request is attributable, reviewable, and controllable.
11. **Evidence-earned autonomy.** The system may gain broader authority as the host grants missions and measured reliability increases.
12. **No reduced consumer destiny.** Consumer distribution is not planned as a permanently less intelligent edition of A.L.I.C.E.

## 4. What makes the product distinct

### 4.1 User-created identity

The product brand, internal codename, and personal assistant identity are separate concepts:

- **Internal codename:** Friday, used by the development team.
- **Public product brand:** selected later through naming and trademark work.
- **Assistant identity:** selected and evolved by the host locally.

The host-selected name is stored inside the encrypted Identity Capsule and can change without changing the product or kernel.

### 4.2 A personal model stack, not one monolithic model

Each installation combines:

- a replaceable general foundation model;
- host memory and knowledge systems;
- learned user, social, causal, and world models;
- host-specific adapters, rankers, and routers;
- a procedural skill library;
- a cognitive executive and action system;
- an evolving host-selected identity.

### 4.3 The Identity Capsule

Each host can export an encrypted, versioned package containing:

- host-selected name and identity state;
- memory, belief, world, and causal graphs;
- learned preferences;
- local adapters and model variants;
- skill manifests;
- evaluation history;
- voice configuration;
- lineage and deletion records.

### 4.4 The Personalization Inspector

The host can ask:

- What do you know about me?
- What are you inferring?
- Why did you make this recommendation?
- Which interactions trained this behavior?
- Which adapter, skill, or memory influenced this answer?
- What would change your mind?
- Delete or retrain without this information.

### 4.5 Proof of non-access

The product should provide stronger evidence than a privacy-policy promise:

- source-verifiable local core where practical;
- signed releases and update manifests;
- visible network egress ledger;
- offline mode and per-capability network controls;
- host-held encryption keys;
- no mandatory cloud account for local use;
- content-free or disabled telemetry defaults;
- local audit, export, deletion, and training-lineage tools.

### 4.6 Host-local evolution

Every interaction and observation can become a learning candidate. The system selectively promotes useful evidence into memories, beliefs, skills, datasets, or model changes using evaluation and lineage. Raw host data does not need to leave the device.

### 4.7 Full personal-AI capability fabric

The consumer product inherits the complete shared capability fabric: models, tools, APIs, GUI control, coding, simulations, scientific systems, devices, and future agents. Host hardware and permissions determine active capability, not a permanently reduced product plan.

## 5. Product forms

### Stage 1 — Windows application

A signed installer provides onboarding, user-selected naming, local model management, data ingestion, chat, memory inspection, learning controls, and a capability dashboard.

### Stage 2 — Cross-platform personal cognitive application

Windows, macOS, and Linux share the same kernel and Identity Capsule format.

### Stage 3 — Persistent personal agent

The system operates as an optional local service with voice, background missions, desktop actions, self-generated skills, and cross-device continuity.

### Stage 4 — Personal AI operating environment

The product becomes an AI-native operating environment or operating-system layer with applications, permissions, identity, memory, action, model execution, and device control designed around the host's personal intelligence.

## 6. Product boundaries

The product does not ship Rayan's data, personality, private tools, or A.L.I.C.E. identity. It ships the same **capability destination** with a blank host identity and local enrollment path.

The difference between deployments is:

- host identity and data;
- product maturity and release timing;
- active hardware profile;
- host-granted authority;
- installed capabilities and local models;
- owner-specific private integrations.

The difference is not a permanent intelligence ceiling.

## 7. Long-term stewardship

Before a dedicated consumer-product team exists, A.L.I.C.E. and the consumer distribution are developed together through the shared kernel.

After launch and creation of a capable maintenance team:

- the consumer team owns packaging, compatibility, updates, support, distribution, hardware coverage, and downstream product releases;
- the shared kernel remains the capability source of truth;
- successful generalized A.L.I.C.E. research enters the shared-kernel backlog;
- Rayan may return primary attention to A.L.I.C.E. without freezing consumer intelligence development;
- the consumer product continues toward full parity through a documented upstream/downstream process.


## 8. Mission Graph and Cognitive Workspace parity

Friday receives the same generalizable Mission Graph and Cognitive Workspace destination as A.L.I.C.E.: nested missions, typed nodes and edges, semantic routing, Result Capsules, traceback, attention explanations, workspace projections, adaptive composition, speaker context, and guest grants. Shared semantic behavior is host-neutral; product branding, defaults, integrations, and release maturity remain product-specific.

The interface follows a no-empty-slot invariant: it renders only useful task projections that exist. Canonical mission state is not stored in the frontend. A.L.I.C.E.'s learned preferences never become Friday defaults.

## 9. Independent repository and dual approval

Friday product source remains in an independent repository from its first implementation commit. A future Friday team is free to invent, research, prototype, test, and propose product-specific work, but may not promote new production behavior without both an exact-artifact A.L.I.C.E. audit attestation and Rayan's explicit approval. Emergency rollback and disablement remain available without introducing new behavior.
