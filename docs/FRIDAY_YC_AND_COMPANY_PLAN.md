# Friday YC and Company Plan

## 1. Company narrative

Friday is a local-first personal cognitive system that becomes different for every host. It learns from the host's private digital life on the host's own hardware, builds inspectable memories and beliefs, and eventually trains host-specific model components without giving the developer access to raw personal data.

A.L.I.C.E. is the founder's flagship implementation and technical proof. Friday is the scalable product.

## 2. Competitive truth

The market already contains local model launchers, local chat applications, memory layers, and products claiming personal models. The pitch must therefore avoid claiming that no personalized or local AI exists.

The stronger claim is:

> Existing products generally specialize in local inference, chat memory, or vendor-managed personalization. Friday is designed as a complete host-owned learning system: local multimodal ingestion, selective lifelong memory, inspectable beliefs, executable skills, host-specific adapters, portable identity, and proof-oriented developer non-access.

## 3. YC timing

As of July 27, 2026, Y Combinator's official application page lists the Fall 2026 on-time deadline as July 27 at 8:00 PM Pacific Time and says later applications are still considered without a promised decision date. YC's Early Decision program allows students to apply for a future batch and be funded immediately if accepted.

## 4. Application positioning

### Problem

General AI services are weakly personalized, while deeper personalization often requires entrusting sensitive life data to a third party. Local model apps protect prompts but usually do not become a continuously learning personal intelligence.

### Solution

Friday installs on the user's computer, ingests approved personal media and documents locally, forms a private personal model stack, and keeps raw personal state encrypted under host-controlled keys.

### Why now

- capable quantized open models increasingly run on consumer PCs;
- Windows ARM, GPU, and NPU execution options are expanding;
- parameter-efficient personalization is practical before full-model training;
- users increasingly expect AI memory but distrust centralized data collection;
- local runtimes and model ecosystems reduce infrastructure cost.

### Technical moat

- A.L.I.C.E. as a deeply developed single-host proving ground;
- provenance-aware memory and deletion;
- experience-to-learning pipeline;
- host-specific evaluation and adapters;
- model-independent Identity Capsule;
- local capability/action architecture;
- proof-oriented privacy and egress controls.

## 5. Demo sequence

The strongest YC demo is not a generic chat screen.

1. Show a clean Friday installation or product prototype.
2. Grant access to a synthetic host folder containing documents, photos, audio, and project files.
3. Show an owner-selected device-resident ingestion profile and verify zero outbound content traffic.
4. Ask a question requiring connections across modalities.
5. Show the memory/belief evidence behind the answer.
6. Correct a preference and show immediate behavior change.
7. Export the Identity Capsule or switch base models without losing identity.
8. Show two host instances answering differently from the same base model.
9. Show A.L.I.C.E. as the mature flagship that supplied the generalized technology.

## 6. Pre-application deliverables

- 60–90 second product demo;
- simple landing page and waitlist;
- architecture diagram;
- two synthetic-host personalization comparison;
- local privacy/egress demonstration;
- installable technical prototype or scripted installer demo;
- concise founder story and execution timeline;
- evidence of development velocity from A.L.I.C.E.;
- 10–20 user discovery interviews focused on privacy and personalization;
- name-clearance status and fallback brand candidates.

## 7. Initial user segment

Start with users who have both high personal-data value and willingness to run local software:

- researchers and engineers;
- founders and independent professionals;
- students with large personal knowledge bases;
- privacy-conscious power users;
- creators with extensive private archives.

Do not begin by claiming the product serves everyone equally. Win one segment with a clearly better personal workflow, then broaden.

## 8. Business model candidates

Potential models that preserve local ownership:

- paid signed desktop application;
- premium model and capability packs;
- one-time license plus paid major upgrades;
- optional encrypted sync/backup subscription;
- enterprise deployment and support;
- marketplace revenue from verified skills;
- dedicated Friday hardware or OS distribution later.

The core local product should not require selling user data or inspecting personal content.
