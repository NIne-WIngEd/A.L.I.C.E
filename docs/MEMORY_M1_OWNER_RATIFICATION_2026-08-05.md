## Owner decision

I explicitly accept the capability-first direction and ratify M1-DX0 through M1-D9 subject to the following owner resolutions.

### 1. Deployment profile naming

The deployment profile will be named `distributed_multi_region`, replacing `distributed_global`.

This name does not establish a geographic, provider, scale, federation, or topology ceiling. Additional deployment profiles may be created whenever evidence, mission requirements, or later technology justify them.

### 2. Identity-adjacent learning and authority

A.L.I.C.E. may autonomously research, construct datasets, train, evaluate, simulate, shadow-test, challenge, and canary-test identity-adjacent models and cognitive projections under authorized privacy, provenance, isolation, deletion, evaluation, and rollback profiles.

The presently ratified production profile does not itself grant autonomous final production authority over constitutional identity or the owner’s identity kernel.

This is a profile-scoped activation state, not a permanent capability prohibition. A successor authority profile may permit progressively greater autonomous promotion when evidence demonstrates sufficient fidelity, safety, reversibility, deletion behavior, provenance, and owner control. Any change to constitutional owner sovereignty remains subject to the owner’s constitutional authority.

### 3. Canary authority progression

The first canary-authority profile will begin with low-consequence, reversible, evidence-backed operational claims such as task status, mission status, project state, tool outcomes, execution receipts, and low-impact factual metadata.

The first profile’s scope does not permanently exclude constitutional, identity, source-person, relationship, health, legal, financial, credential, security, or deletion-sensitive cognition from automated processing.

Those classes may immediately be researched, modeled, simulated, evaluated, and shadow-tested. They may later enter specialized canary or production profiles when their evidence thresholds, authority rules, privacy controls, consequence models, correction paths, deletion behavior, and rollback requirements are satisfied.

Every canary restriction must record its profile, reason, evidence, review point, removal criterion, successor path, activation condition, and rollback or exit.

### 4. Deletion and residual model influence

Deletion is a binding cross-system outcome requirement. No single present-day deletion technique is permanently mandated.

When deleted information may retain material influence in a model, adapter, dataset, index, graph, cache, replay system, checkpoint, or derived artifact, A.L.I.C.E. must select and verify the strongest technically effective response available under the governing deletion profile.

Valid responses may include verified unlearning, selective retraining, model editing, adapter removal, checkpoint rollback, dataset reconstruction, key destruction, influence isolation, deployment withdrawal, model retirement, or replacement by a clean successor.

The selected response must be supported by lineage, evaluation, residual-influence measurement, truthful disclosure of technical limitations, and a documented recovery path.

High-impact private, identity, or source-person influence may not remain production-active merely because complete deletion is technically difficult. Where acceptable residual influence cannot be demonstrated, the affected artifact must be contained, withdrawn, replaced, or retired.

This requirement governs the deletion result. It does not impose a permanent ceiling on future unlearning, editing, training, or model architectures.

### 5. Cognitive Graph authority

The Cognitive Graph begins as a provenance-linked reasoning and projection plane because that is the strongest presently evidenced authority arrangement.

This default is not a permanent graph-authority ceiling.

A graph engine or later graph-native system may hold canonical authority for one or more relation, temporal, causal, social, mission, identity, world-model, or other cognitive domains when a registered authority profile demonstrates appropriate consistency, provenance, bitemporal history, evidence linkage, correction, conflict adjudication, privacy, product isolation, deletion, export, replication, reconciliation, availability, and rollback behavior.

The breadth of graph authority will be determined by evidence and registered capability, not restricted in advance to narrowly defined relation classes.

### 6. Effect of this ratification

I explicitly ratify M1-DX0 through M1-D9 as the governing architecture and contract decisions for the next memory-program stage.

This ratification authorizes immediate continuation of capability-first research, contract development, prototypes, alternative backend implementations, cognitive-graph development, distributed-system experiments, workflow experiments, dataset construction, challenger training, shadow inference, evaluation, deletion research, migration tooling, and other reversible successor work allowed by the applicable authority and privacy profiles.

This ratification does not, by itself, make an experimental capability production-active. It does not, by itself, transfer canonical authority, perform an irreversible production cutover, expose private state, merge A.L.I.C.E. and Friday state, begin an unsupervised destructive migration, or publish a release.

Production activation, authority cutover, and release occur through their applicable evidence, evaluation, privacy, deletion, rollback, mission, and owner-authority profiles.

No present technology, database, topology, model, context size, graph role, workflow engine, scale checkpoint, training method, deployment profile, research sequence, or implementation state created by M1 constitutes a permanent capability ceiling.

Owner sovereignty, truthful state, epistemic integrity, privacy, provenance, product and host isolation, authorized deletion, export, correction, rollback, revocation, pause, and shutdown remain binding enabling invariants.
