# Product-Family Capability Parity Standard

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **OWNER-RATIFIED FLAGSHIP CAPABILITY RULE:** A.L.I.C.E. is the flagship and mandatory default capability upstream. Through at least completion of A.L.I.C.E. Phase 15, Friday must receive every transferable A.L.I.C.E. capability. Friday may gain a new capability only after A.L.I.C.E. has implemented, evaluated, approved, and gained it, unless MK Rayan records an explicit exact-scope owner override.
>
> This owner-ratified rule supersedes conflicting capability-order, team-independence, or Phase 6.5 repository-creation language in this document.


**Status:** Ratified architecture standard

## 1. Rule

Every capability accepted into the A.L.I.C.E. destination architecture must enter the shared-kernel capability catalog and consumer-product backlog unless it is personal content or an owner-specific integration rather than a generalizable capability.

The consumer product may lag A.L.I.C.E. for productization. It may not be defined as a permanently reduced-intelligence edition.

## 3. Allowed reasons for release lag

A capability may ship later in the consumer product because it still requires:

- host-neutral interfaces;
- hardware-aware implementation;
- migration and rollback support;
- local privacy design;
- packaging and update support;
- objective evaluation;
- support-team readiness;
- user-facing controls;
- legal distribution work.

These are delivery gates, not architectural prohibitions.

## 4. Parity ledger

The product family maintains a machine-readable parity ledger for every capability:

- capability identifier;
- A.L.I.C.E. status;
- shared-kernel status;
- consumer-product status;
- reason for any lag;
- generalization owner;
- target phase;
- evidence required for promotion.

A capability cannot disappear from the consumer roadmap merely because A.L.I.C.E. implemented it privately first.

## 5. Stewardship after team separation

When a dedicated consumer team exists:

- the kernel team maintains shared contracts and evaluation suites;
- the A.L.I.C.E. track explores frontier capabilities;
- the consumer team productizes and maintains downstream releases;
- parity debt is reviewed as a product and technical metric;
- Rayan may focus on A.L.I.C.E. while the consumer team preserves the parity commitment.


## 6. Cognitive Workspace working-parity lane

The following generalizable capabilities receive immediate kernel and Friday disposition: Mission Graph, semantic routing, Result Capsules, traceback, attention policy, workspace projection, adaptive compositor, host window overrides, speaker context, guest sessions, guest grants, and legacy-chat compatibility.

A.L.I.C.E. may evaluate frontier variants first. Friday productization planning begins immediately. Product releases remain independent, and each records its own privacy, host-isolation, evaluation, A.L.I.C.E.-audit, and Rayan-approval state.

## 7. Production-governance parity

A dedicated Friday team may independently manage maintenance, product experience, packaging, support, and productization of capabilities already eligible through A.L.I.C.E. A Friday-originated capability that A.L.I.C.E. lacks must remain an upstream proposal until A.L.I.C.E. has implemented, evaluated, approved, and gained it, unless Rayan records an explicit exact-scope override. The team may not silently alter shared semantics or unilaterally ship production behavior. Exact-artifact dual approval and emergency rollback boundaries are part of the product-family contract.
