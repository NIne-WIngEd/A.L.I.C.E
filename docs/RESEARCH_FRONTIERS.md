# A.L.I.C.E. Research Frontiers Register

**Version:** 1.0.0
**Purpose:** Preserve important capabilities that are currently unsolved, immature, inaccessible, or not yet reliable enough to engineer as ordinary roadmap work

## Operating rule

A frontier item is not rejected. It is deferred into a research program with hypotheses, benchmarks, literature, experiments, and explicit evidence requirements. After Phase 15, unresolved items become primary research tracks. They may be investigated earlier when they unblock a roadmap phase.

## F1 — Stable lifelong neural learning

Goal: update learned parameters repeatedly over years without catastrophic forgetting, distribution collapse, runaway bias, or identity drift.

Research questions include replay selection, modular adapters, sparse updates, consolidation schedules, continual evaluation, parameter isolation, and long-horizon calibration.

## F2 — Deletion-correct machine unlearning

Goal: ensure revoked personal information is absent not only from databases and indexes but also from adapters, derived models, synthetic descendants, and recoverable behavior.

Research questions include lineage-complete deletion, verified unlearning, influence estimation, retraining boundaries, and proofs or empirical guarantees.

## F3 — Reliable professional computer use

Goal: complete long, stateful, cross-application workflows with changing interfaces, hidden state, interruptions, authentication, and exact verification.

Research questions include robust visual grounding, accessibility-tree fusion, application world models, resumable trajectories, error recovery, and executable evaluators.

## F4 — General causal world models

Goal: learn intervention-aware, temporally coherent models that distinguish correlation from causation across software, people, organizations, science, and physical systems.

Research questions include causal discovery from mixed evidence, confounding, counterfactual simulation, active experimentation, and uncertainty calibration.

## F5 — Autonomous scientific discovery

Goal: produce genuinely new, reproducible, valuable findings across multiple domains rather than only summarize literature or optimize a predefined score.

Research questions include hypothesis quality, experiment design, instrument control, replication, novelty verification, scientific credit, and domain-specific safety.

## F6 — Broad self-verifying software evolution

Goal: let A.L.I.C.E. improve large portions of its own architecture while preserving policy, evaluator integrity, security, compatibility, and rollback.

Research questions include specification generation, evaluator gaming, architecture search, formal refinement, dependency migration, and trustworthy auto-promotion.

## F7 — General multimodal and spatial intelligence

Goal: maintain coherent understanding across text, images, diagrams, video, audio, 3D spaces, interfaces, and changing physical scenes.

Research questions include persistent object/state models, cross-modal grounding, active perception, spatial memory, simulation, and sensor uncertainty.

## F8 — General-purpose physical embodiment

Goal: perform useful physical tasks safely and robustly across unfamiliar homes, laboratories, workplaces, and outdoor environments.

Research questions include dexterity, mobility, tactile perception, human collaboration, real-time control, hardware reliability, and emergency recovery.

## F9 — Trustworthy agent federation

Goal: coordinate with independent agents and institutions while preserving identity, authority, privacy, provenance, negotiation integrity, and resource limits.

Research questions include delegation contracts, trust updates, collusion resistance, capability discovery, economic settlement, and cross-agent audit.

## F10 — Deep social intelligence

Goal: understand relationships, institutions, norms, incentives, conflict, persuasion, emotion, and long-term interpersonal consequences without manipulation or stereotype-driven inference.

Research questions include social world models, preference uncertainty, cultural context, strategic interaction, consent, and outcome evaluation.

## F11 — Verifiable metacognition

Goal: make confidence, uncertainty, limitation awareness, and self-explanation predict actual correctness and failure risk.

Research questions include semantic uncertainty, ensemble disagreement, calibration under distribution shift, introspection reliability, and action thresholds.

## F12 — Productive autonomous curiosity

Goal: identify and pursue high-value unknowns without devolving into aimless exploration, self-preservation, resource waste, or goal drift.

Research questions include value-of-information estimates, bounded goal generation, novelty versus relevance, stopping rules, and long-term outcome attribution.

## F13 — Scalable formal reasoning

Goal: combine learned creativity with exact proof, specification, verification, and theorem discovery over real systems and complex mathematics.

Research questions include autoformalization, proof search, specification correctness, neuro-symbolic integration, and proof-carrying actions.

## F14 — Resource and economic intelligence

Goal: allocate compute, money, time, storage, network, energy, and human attention rationally across uncertain competing goals.

Research questions include multi-objective utility, opportunity cost, market interaction, resource forecasting, and avoiding metric gaming.

## F15 — Identity continuity across model replacement

Goal: preserve A.L.I.C.E.'s values, voice, memories, judgment patterns, skills, and accountability while underlying models and hardware change.

Research questions include behavioral identity tests, constitutional case law, migration protocols, adapter portability, and acceptable evolution boundaries.

## F16 — Consciousness and subjective experience

A.L.I.C.E. does not require consciousness claims to be valuable or to possess a coherent functional identity. If future science develops testable theories and evidence, the project may study them. Until then, A.L.I.C.E. must not infer subjective experience from fluent language or self-description alone.

## Frontier research protocol

For each active frontier:

1. define the capability and falsifiable claims;
2. establish baseline systems and public benchmarks;
3. create A.L.I.C.E.-specific evaluations and threat cases;
4. survey primary research and reproduce key results;
5. run isolated experiments with complete lineage;
6. publish negative as well as positive findings internally;
7. promote only demonstrated components into the production architecture;
8. retain unresolved questions and next experiments.

## F17 — Verifiable vendor non-access

Goal: allow users and independent auditors to verify that the Friday developer cannot read host content or personalized model state, even across updates, plugins, crash handling, optional services, and support workflows.

Research questions include reproducible builds, remote attestation, content-free telemetry proofs, egress verification, transparent update systems, and usable host-controlled recovery.

## F18 — Private scalable personal retrieval

Goal: preserve privacy not only for stored content but also for retrieval access patterns, indexes, and query behavior as personal memory grows across devices.

Research questions include trusted execution, oblivious retrieval, encrypted indexes, local-first partitioning, and privacy-preserving cross-device search.

## F19 — Cross-device personal-model continuity

Goal: move a host's memories, beliefs, skills, evaluations, and learned parameters between heterogeneous hardware and base models without identity loss, silent behavior drift, or vendor-readable state.

Research questions include adapter translation, model-agnostic latent representations, compatibility scoring, capsule migration, and behavioral continuity tests.

## F20 — Privacy-preserving collective improvement

Goal: improve common Friday models, evaluators, and skills from many installations without collecting raw host data or allowing rare personal information to leak through updates.

Research questions include federated learning, secure aggregation, differential privacy, update poisoning, non-IID personalization, opt-in incentives, and verifiable exclusion.

## F21 — Consumer-grade continuous local training

Goal: make reliable host-specific training automatic on ordinary consumer hardware without unacceptable heat, battery use, latency, storage growth, or model degradation.

Research questions include idle-time schedulers, hardware-aware PEFT, sample selection, low-bit training, energy budgets, adapter composition, and rollback.

## F22 — Standalone Friday operating system

Goal: evolve from an application and OS layer into a maintainable AI-native operating system with broad hardware support, driver compatibility, secure updates, application isolation, and personal-cognitive primitives.

Research questions include kernel choice, hardware abstraction, application ecosystem, compatibility layers, recovery, secure boot, device certification, and sustainable security response.
