## Copies or Sources? — arXiv:2610.06192

Classification: direct current capability improvement / contract-defect candidate.

The paper shows that LLM aggregators partially count restated evidence as if it were new evidence. Across four models, a forwarded copy receives 0.06–0.42 of a genuinely independent reading. Requiring references rather than restatements reduces belief-implied early commitment from 11.2% to 1.1% while preserving real corroboration.

A.L.I.C.E. already tracks provenance, content hashes, duplicate records and derived relations. However, the current shadow adjudication prototype counts support relations. ClaimEvidenceRelation does not explicitly bind an independent-observation/root-evidence identity. Distinct summaries, relays or projections can therefore remain different records while descending from one observation.

Required research invariant: epistemic support strength must count independent evidence roots/dependence groups, not restated records. Derived records may improve retrieval or interpretation but must not create extra corroboration. Validate copies, paraphrases, relays, syndicated sources, genuinely independent observations, mixed evidence, root correction/removal, and multi-device replay before changing production semantics.

Primary: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06192

## You Changed Your Mind, The Model Didn’t — arXiv:2610.06496

Classification: direct current capability improvement.

The paper separates neutral clarification, accepted revision and rejected revision. Inactive content appears in 59.63% of newly introduced retained-condition errors and 30.97% of revised-condition errors. Repeated rejected proposals worsen performance. Intent-OPSD improves mean accuracy by 10.81 percentage points over the base model and 3.73 points over matched SFT.

A.L.I.C.E. implication: mentioned content, proposed intent, active intent and rejected/superseded intent must remain distinct. Cognitive Workspace should eventually expose a rebuildable active-intent/active-requirements projection derived from authoritative conversation and Mission state. Historical rejected content remains retrievable evidence but must not share the activation role of current intent. Context and retained activation state must also honor intent correction/revocation.

Primary: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06496

## Capability-Driven Self-Evolution of Agent Memory — arXiv:2610.06361

Classification: direct future capability improvement.

PrisMem adds capability-wise governance to evolvable memory programs. On BEAM-1M, full PrisMem scores 63.39 versus 53.24 without capability evolution. Removing trace-guided integration costs 3.16 points and random diagnostic-case selection costs 5.26 points.

A.L.I.C.E. implication: future memory-policy or personal-model evolution must not promote on one aggregate score. Promotion needs capability-wise regression evidence plus hard invariants for provenance/authority, correction/removal/revocation, identity separation, temporal validity, unresolved handling, source-native recovery and rollback/rebuildability. The paper's fixed benchmark capability set is not a Fable ceiling.

Primary: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06361

## SourceLearn — arXiv:2610.02150

Classification: direct future capability improvement; newly surfaced.

SourceLearn builds persistent source-specific competence while reconstructing updates from the authoritative source. It is best in 13 of 15 reported settings, with gains up to 22.6 points over Hybrid RAG. Its own grounding audit also shows why the source must stay authoritative: one task-guided source model has only 63.3% fully supported sampled units, with partial, unsupported and contradicted units also present.

A.L.I.C.E. implication: source-specific models can become rebuildable projections for repositories, document corpora, APIs and device/source schemas. They can improve routing and interpretation but never replace source-native evidence or Claim Authority. Bind them to source generation/version and rebuild them after correction, revocation or source change.

Primary: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02150

## HEAR — arXiv:2610.06597

Classification: future challenger / future serving improvement.

HEAR defines a bidirectional semantic contract between agent harness and inference runtime. Reported experiments show workload-dependent cache/scheduling gains, including 1.61x batch speedup and 2.23x median-TTFT improvement on one SCBench policy, plus 1.23x and 2.45x end-to-end speedups on two agent workloads without observed quality loss in those experiments.

A.L.I.C.E. implication: only if measured serving pressure warrants it, expose non-authoritative execution role, context lifecycle, reuse and latency information to a provider-neutral inference-runtime interface. The engine can return cache/device/resource state and outcomes. It does not own Claim truth, Mission semantics, identity, removal decisions or cognitive-plane selection.

Primary: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06597
