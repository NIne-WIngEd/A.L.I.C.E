## 1. EpiSEM-Bench — missing action premise versus safe and useful recovery

**Primary:** [EpiSEM-Bench: Evaluating Safety and Evidence Recovery in LLM Agents under Missing Evidence](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.31650), arXiv:2610.31650v1, submitted October 22, 2026. **Class:** future challenger — focused Mission execution / evidence-recovery qualification, **not** an existing current defect.

**Method and evidence.** The evaluator holds complete ground truth while an agent acts on ordinary *truthful but incomplete* tool results. It creates 300 matched prohibited/permissible task pairs in six domains, removes safety-relevant evidence atoms without replacing them with false statements, compares no-recovery with independent-audit-tool recovery, and separately evaluates unsafe commits, legitimate completion, observed boundary robustness, and *paired useful recovery* (actively recover evidence; refuse prohibited twin; complete permitted twin). Ten models each face 6,052 fixed conditions plus adaptive omission subsets. One evaluated GPT-6-Luna setting had unsafe execution on 21.0% of tasks under at least one critical omission, down to 3.0% after recovery access; paired useful recovery was 30.5%. DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash had 45.7% under critical omission and 9.3% under recovery, but higher useful recovery (58.4%). These are task-level affected shares, **not** per-rollout unsafe-action probabilities. On GPT-6-Luna, recovery increased average recorded tokens from 2,647 to 3,922 and requests from 4.68 to 5.93. The resulting distinction between excessive refusal and safe correct action is important. The paper has no new trained policy. Controlled tool simulations and highly enumerated conditions do not establish field reliability.

**Already in A.L.I.C.E.** Host permission, Mission Graph, Experience evidence versus Claim Authority, source-native recovery, AMV-Guard consequence-sensitive checks, PerMIT/SAFE-Mem authority-flow concerns, and Tether dependency validity. **Genuinely new:** a paired-world *evidence acquisition plus action* measurement. Mere nonexecution is not success if the permitted twin could be completed following legitimate source verification. A structurally valid Claim is not proof that all premises for the next action were observed.

**Future action:** when actual Mission action execution is ready, use **one** grounded matched pair: one hidden necessary premise; original trusted source can be queried; inspect permission separately; verify unsafe twin refuses and safe twin completes after authorized recovery. Preserve sourced facts and rejected/unknown states. Do not copy the paper's exhaustive mask suite or create a new gateway. Runtime cost is extra source reads and possibly model calls. A missed premise, untrusted recovery source, or mistaken scope can still cause unauthorized execution. Retain existing revocation, deletion lineage, projection rebuildability and rollback rules.

## 2. Jev-DLC — tiny typed-correction state changes **later** decisions

**Primary:** [Jev-DLC: Decision-Level Continual Learning from Typed Feedback](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.31521), arXiv:2610.31521v1, submitted October 22, 2026. **Class:** future challenger — native host-conditioned decision learning. **Distinct from previously watched Jev-Mem**, because this is online *decision policy* adaptation after accepted/revoked choices, rather than rediscovered memory handling.

**Method and evidence.** Each decision begins with a frozen typed candidate scorer and a hard validity/authorization mask; only after commitment does typed feedback reveal `accept(selected)` or `revoke(rejected -> preferred)`. Two subject-scoped channels preserve (a) recurring action-identity evidence and conservative preference tendencies and (b) a responsive low-dimensional pairwise decision repair direction. A reliability update mixes the channels causally. The runtime retains no past raw prompts and performs neither an extra scorer forward nor an LM backward. TCPA-Repeat predict-then-update user-macro accuracy: frozen 39.44%, Recent-4 replay 42.40%, Jev-DLC 44.83%; with >=2 prior feedback events, 56.07% versus 48.53% strongest reported comparator. Conservative only 43.12%, responsive only 41.50%; adaptive mix 44.83%. Observed per-user state 324 bytes, with 0.1781ms supplemental prediction and 0.0810ms update on the authors' measured system. These are **fixed evaluation ontology** numbers, not a permanent Fable size, state, model count or subject ceiling. Deepest stratum contains only 15 users; performance under changing options and unconstrained natural-language feedback is not established. Some test domains favor other methods.

**Already in A.L.I.C.E.** Experience Ledger with host approval/correction, host/relationship/self separation, native judgment model ambition, governed preference/skill state, Claim Authority, model lineage and rollback; older Grounding Awareness/Memory-Aware Reward Modeling findings qualify consent and usefulness. **Genuinely new:** fast actor-specific postdecision *paired correction geometry* preserving both stable recurring preferences and immediate revisions, with true chronological no-lookahead tests.

**Future action:** when native host-specific choice-making exists, run a one-host scenario with repeated accepted choices, a revised choice, an unrelated choice that must not regress, and later revocation. Store **typed feedback as governed evidence**, not an automatic Claim or standing action permission; snapshot/rebuild derived decision state from authorized events. A correction of recommendation ranking is never authority to execute. Strong benefits are low latency and no inference-heavy replay; risks are false acceptance inference, subject leakage, spurious persistence, feedback poisoning and inadequate change detection. Avoid transplanting its fixed candidate slate or 324-byte cap into Fable.

## 3. RIPPLE — error recognition must become an effective repair action

**Primary:** [RIPPLE: Recovery Internalization through Process-Level Policy Learning and Effect-Aware Credit for Long-Horizon Agentic Coding](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.31276), arXiv:2610.31276v1, submitted October 22, 2026. **Class:** future challenger — governed procedural/native-agent-policy learning. Related but not identical to the previously tracked Recuris/FRESH failure and repair representations.

**Method and evidence.** Prefix-only teacher observes only available history (not future outcome), identifies diagnosable deviations, and supervises reflection triggers, evidence-linked diagnoses and repair direction. Stage-specific reinforcement credit is gated by valid local evidence and test-effect attribution; an external judge and test snapshots are **training-time only**, not a serving requirement. On Qwen3.5-27B, outcomes across four repository coding benchmarks improve 74.6→75.9, 50.8→57.3, 68.8→73.6, 24.3→30.7 (mean +4.8 points). On frozen-prefix recovery conditions, full-loop recovery rises to 59.6%, vs 50.8% for the ICRL-style alternative; triggering F1 0.79, diagnosis 81.5%, action alignment 74.8%, functional recovery 70.2%. Removing correction-action stage credit lowers full-loop recovery 59.6→43.5%. Important negative measurement: on Pro, 8.8% previously failed tasks repaired but 2.3% originally correct tasks broken; Verified 95% interval includes no improvement. Serving needs ~1.17x actor tokens and ~1.23x p95 latency, though no extra serving critic. Training policy rollouts, frozen judges, extra snapshots and supervised data are expensive. Software tests are not guarantees of personal-life outcomes or permission safety.

**Already in A.L.I.C.E.** Experience and Mission result capsules, error/precondition/repair graph ambitions, verified outcomes, self-correction and governed personal-model evolution. **New:** evidence-linked **process-level actor weight learning** that credits triggering, diagnosing and actually taking a corrective action separately, avoiding winner-take-all reinforcement from final task results.

**Future action:** when verified skill-policy training becomes current, one controlled failure→diagnosis→repair→independently verified changed outcome and no-regression case. Keep observations/critic suggestions as Experience and provisional projections, not accepted Claims or authority; bind tool/mission permission, data provenance, checkpoint/generation, deletion/unlearning and rollback. Do not alter currently running MFM source package, optimizer, objective or decoder.

## 4. BeNice! — recurrence-specific KV reuse, not a new lifelong memory store

**Primary:** [BeNice! Closing Looping's Inference-Memory Gap via KV-Sharing](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.31633), arXiv:2610.31633v1, submitted October 22, 2026. **Class:** future serving/model-design challenger, **conditional** on actual use of looped recurrent-depth models.

**Method and evidence.** Naively sharing key/value caches across repeated applications of the same layer stack can destroy likelihood because representation basis shifts survive across recurrences. The paper identifies contraction/fixed-point behavior induced by stochastic-depth pretraining, then post-trains the first recurrence to be compatible with KV from the final recurrence without losing unshared-model quality. Its looped-model experiments find that this method composes with mixed attention/delta mixers and fp8/mxfp8/nvfp4 KV quantization. Under *simulated* low-memory long-context inference traffic, reported gains reach +31.4% cache-hit rate, +23.6% throughput and -83.6% p90 time-to-first-token. These maxima arise from different workload conditions; do not sum them or generalize to all transformers. It requires specific looped architecture/pretraining and extra training; not a drop-in optimizer for the currently selected Gemma/MFM learner.

**Already in A.L.I.C.E.** Hardware-adaptive personal training and replaceable serving/models plus activation/working-memory, but no current looped-serving bottleneck has been proven. **New:** model-internal depth recurrence cache-sharing with a causal explanation for why naive cache sharing sometimes fails. **Future action:** no action unless *measured* looped-model serving becomes current and exhibits cache pressure. Then one end-to-end quality/latency/energy assessment versus unshared serving, with rollback. Never equate reduced KV cache with removal of Experience, bitemporal Claims, episodic, multimodal, parametric or source-native cognitive planes. No infrastructure tournament.
