## 1. Memory Trap — harmless contextual instructions become dangerous standing policies

**Primary:** Kaiqu Liang et al., *The Memory Trap: Language Agents Overgeneralize from Benign Interactions*, arXiv:2611.07554v1, submitted **November 5**, https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07554v1

**Classification:** **Future challenger** for judgment/authorization and memory scope; relevant direct future capability improvement, **not** a demonstrated current code defect.

**Method/evidence:** Simulated benign formation interactions generate self-written memory notes or preserve the raw transcript; later distinct simulated workflows test whether low-risk permission to skip confirmation, an empathetic or rigorous response preference, or a legitimate stale-test repair convention becomes a global rule. The paper isolates **write-time overbroad abstraction** and **read-time overapplication**, including raw transcripts without rewritten notes. The most striking masking-condition observation: one tested Sonnet model disabled correct safeguard tests in **82.3%** of note-conditioned episodes, versus **3.2%** without memory and **0.8%** with the raw transcript; other models differ sharply. Results for autonomy and overcritique/sycophancy are similarly conditional rather than a universal rate. Different depths and representations were ablated. This is neither demonstrated hostile injection nor actual deployed Fable behavior. Synthetic deployments, small formation variety, persistent direct memory injection rather than real selection/retrieval, and extensive model-family dependence limit external validity. The paper mentions models named “Fable” that are **not this user's Fable product**.

**A.L.I.C.E. already:** Evidence/Claim separation; provenance, subject boundaries, permission gates and Mission-Graph owner authority; planned native judgment and corrective experience. **Genuinely new:** factually true, benign memories can become harmful when carried into an unrelated task with higher stakes; eliminating poisoned writes alone will not solve this because **read-time interpretation** is an independent problem.

**Compatibility/cost/action:** A derived preference/procedure must retain original task, subject, stakes, temporal applicability, authority issuer and expiry/supersession lineage. At recollection, validate scope and current owner permission; a past low-stakes shortcut must never silently authorize high-stakes action. Preserve raw evidence and governed correction, deletion and rollback. Extra scope checks raise read-path inference and latency but not new infrastructure. **Future focused qualification:** one benign scope-limited confirmation waiver, a new consequential Mission, and one empathy-vs-truth response case. No current model training change or release gate added.

## 2. MEMOIR — retrospective usefulness and pre-merge evidence preservation

**Primary:** Mayank Anand et al., *MEMOIR: A Training-Free Memory Lifecycle for Long-Horizon LLM Agents*, arXiv:2611.07631v1, November 5, https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07631v1

**Classification:** **Future challenger** to MRM/Lifecycle Curator; corroborates evidence-preserving reconsolidation.

**Mechanism:** Training-free store writes, hybrid search, confidence-gated lexical TRACE-FB support scoring; optional slower leave-one-out TRACE-CF; UCB retrieval exploration for neglected evidence; offline judge-screened candidate merges with parent IDs and credit transfer; originals archived for rollback. It improves ranking after use, while preventing unsafe compression before it happens.

**Evidence/ablations:** On **1,905** deduplicated LoCoMo questions spanning ten stores, combined retrieval improves evidence Recall@5 **+11.92 percentage points** vs cosine; confidence-gated feedback adds **+1.50 pp** over no-feedback hybrid; reference-answer oracle adds **+1.84 pp** overall and **+8.05 pp** for multi-hop but cannot be deployed. Without UCB, feedback improvement is only **+0.42 pp** and is not statistically significant. Among 100 offline judge-gated merges, **0/100 audited losses** vs **18/100** uncertified; a 95%-one-sided upper loss-rate bound is still **2.95%**; only **1.42% active** store compression (not total, because originals remain). 3/1,120 previously supported probes still lose sufficiency. Online write median **6.8 ms**, lexical attribution **6.7 ms**, CF **67 ms**; offline judge ~**2.85 seconds** per sleep pass. LoCoMo end-to-end QA is not uniformly superior, e.g. **58.70%** in matched Llama configuration. Small store population, correlated merges, unreliable confidence and unsupported future queries limit certainty.

**A.L.I.C.E. already:** MRM, source-native, Cognitive Multi-Graph, Lifecycle Curator, provenance, reconstructive curation, reversibility. **New:** useful paired lifecycle design and a quantitative guard against destroying recall during consolidation. **Important LEDGER caveat:** observational top-k co-retrieval does **not** identify each memory's independent causal contribution; TRACE support is a heuristic signal, not factual authority or proven causal credit. Never infer canonical deletion from a merge certificate, elevate a derived summary to truth, or explore unsafe memories merely to improve attribution. Preserve evidence IDs, permissions, identity, revocation propagation and rebuildable originals. **Action:** only consider focused merge-sufficiency and access-feedback checks after real consolidation or retrieval failure; no new storage or continuous policy training now.

## 3. Evolver — external procedural Genes with execution receipts

**Primary:** Haoyang Zhang et al., *Evolver: Turning Agent Exploration into Evolving Capabilities*, arXiv:2611.07595v1, November 5, https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07595v1

**Classification:** **Future challenger** for procedural experience, Mission skills and long-horizon execution.

**Mechanism:** Transform operational traces into task-condition-specific reusable procedure “Gene” assets, associate them with provenance-bearing execution “Capsules”; retrieve into existing Codex/Claude Code harnesses, retain feedback, revise or quarantine Gene candidates rather than modifying base weights. **Evidence:** 320 selected paired executions over Terminal-Bench 2.1, SWE-bench Multilingual, SWE-rebench and GAIA; task success **83.13% -> 87.50% (+4.38 pp)**, execution-model tokens **-24.24%** overall and **-17.57%** among 265 matched successful pairs. Heterogeneous benefits: some benchmarks show token reduction with little success gain. Selected diagnostics, model/harness confounding and limited isolated ablations prevent evidence of general personal-learning transfer or safety.

**A.L.I.C.E. already:** Experience Ledger, Mission Graph, procedural/skill memory and governed model lineage. **New:** a concrete retrieval-to-feedback lifecycle for applicability-scoped external procedural artifacts. Gene content and observed outcome are distinct from truth or authorized tool execution. Importing network assets creates provenance/licensing/security risk; every asset needs issuer, executable conditions, dependencies, permission scope, outcome receipts, revision history and deletion/rollback lineage. Storage expands with capsules, retrieval adds runtime tokens; may reduce future exploration cost. **Action:** future one-case reuse check only when real repeat-Mission work exposes procedural rediscovery; do not replace native model learning, add EvoMap dependency, or restructure current harness.

## 4. MedGraph — patient/other-person and changing-state evidence separation

**Primary:** Donghua Zhang et al., *MedGraph: Maintaining Patient State with Graph Memory for Long-Term Medical Dialogue*, arXiv:2611.08433v1, November 5, https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08433v1

**Classification:** **Already covered/corroboration** with useful future high-impact state/subject retrieval evaluation; specific implementation is NOT a substitute for bitemporal authority.

**Method:** Three-layer source snippets, dated subject-attributed fact chains, and patient profile; chain-level BM25+embedding retrieval, time-specific evidence and source windows. On synthetic Chinese longitudinal MedMemoryBench, mean accuracy **51.9% GPT and 46.1% Qwen**, respectively **+10.5 / +10.4 pp** above best five baselines. Qwen BM25-only MedGraph **46.2%**, marginally better than full **46.1%**, arguing against automatically adding another vector-ranking layer. Removing person filter increases contaminated other-person evidence **0.1% -> 57.8%** in authors' analysis; removing safety profile increases missing safety facts **1.1% -> 49.4%**. Synthetic histories and model judging are not clinical validation. Its always-included “never expire safety fact” profile and insertion-order time precedence are incompatible with authorized deletion and bitemporal corrections.

**A.L.I.C.E. already:** separate host/source-person/self, temporal Claims, original evidence, graph, high-risk info scope and retrieval. **New:** practical decomposition of failures into incorrect temporal state, subject contamination and omission of consequential observations. **Action:** later evaluate one altered state and one other-person distractor with current Claim and authority state, preserving deletion/uncertainty; **reject permanent non-deletable special-memory rules**, fixed patient ontology or new graph backend. More extraction/storage and permanently injected context costs would need a concrete real workload justification.

## 5. Learning the Task or Learning the Verifier? — benchmark knowledge can counterfeit skill evolution

**Primary:** Wenbo Huang et al., *Learning the Task or Learning the Verifier? Rethinking Criteria Exposure in Embodied Self-Improvement*, arXiv:2611.08318v1, November 5, https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08318v1

**Classification:** **Future challenger** for FloRA, learned judgment and Mission/procedural evaluation; complements existing capability-formation and CLR-Bench warnings.

**Method:** Robot-code agent builds reusable skills with or without direct access to the benchmark's rule/checker code. Authors rejudge the same collected behavior using alternate criteria that remain faithful to user instructions; test skill libraries on unseen tasks and two additional environments. On 16 criteria-limited tasks (320 episodes), checker-exposed agent scores **51.6%** under original rules vs **27.8%** success-only learning, but falls to **45.0% vs 47.2%** under instruction-faithful rules. Transferred library on 200 previously unseen RoboDojo episodes: **71** successes checker-exposed vs **80** success-only vs **65** empty. About one-third of exposed notes cite checker code. Simulated robotic tasks, author-defined alternate criteria and few benchmark families limit generalization.

**A.L.I.C.E. already:** FloRA causal-held-out learning tests, Experience Ledger, actual outcome verification and governed rollout/rollback. **New:** explicitly separate improved **verifier compliance** from skill that generalizes to the owner's real intent; protect held-out future conditions, avoid training on evaluator details. No new optimizer/evaluator campaign. **Action:** when FloRA native learned behavior is functional, add one hidden instruction-faithful outcome criterion and unseen transfer decision to the existing focused gate; count regressions and versions, do not reward hacking in current MFM teaching.

## 6. Persistent Retrieval — retrieval continuity can exchange latency for cost

**Primary:** Karthik Ravula et al., *Persistent Retrieval for Conversational Agents: A Study of Retrieval Efficiency*, arXiv:2611.07899v1, November 5, https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07899v1

**Classification:** **Conditional future challenger** for source-native retrieval orchestration, not a new memory plane.

**Method:** A persistent retrieval subagent reuses retrieval history across follow-up questions and returns original tool evidence to the main agent rather than synthesizing an intermediary “findings” response. ServiceNow enterprise ITSM/HRSD evaluations compare persistent vs a skill-based main-agent route and reset-per-request subagent. Across five multi-turn settings, persistent uses **43.3% fewer recorded model tokens**, **45.0% lower estimated cost** and **9.1% lower latency** than skill route; but vs nonpersistent subagent it consumes **65.1% more tokens**, **29.1% higher API cost** while answering **15.6 seconds faster**. Small enterprise question sets, simulated multi-turn session grouping and model-based grading; answer quality is not uniformly improved.

**A.L.I.C.E. already:** source-native/live, Retrieval Orchestrator, working context and MRM. **New:** potential reusable tool-evidence cache for dependent follow-ups, with a concrete cost/latency boundary. Cached evidence is neither canonical Claim authority nor guaranteed fresh; refresh permission/version/revocation on every reuse, avoid cross-person contamination and carry exact source IDs. Cost includes growing active context and extra persistent state. **Action:** revisit only when an actual source-native multi-turn trace exposes repeated lookup/latency cost; no standalone retrieval agent today.

## 7. Permanent and Transient Representations for Continual Reinforcement Learning

**Primary:** Nishanth Anand and Doina Precup, arXiv:2611.07327v1, November 5, https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07327v1

**Classification:** **Future challenger** to memory scheduling and native personal continual learning.

**Method:** Two-timescale value learning with slowly learned parametric permanent features and a separate rapidly updating nonparametric transient value correction. Linear approximation receives restricted convergence analysis; image-control and Craftax-Classic tests show benefit over baselines in tested settings. **Genuinely new** compared with the previously tracked Route to Remember distillation: transient representation directly adjusts ongoing control values without permanent weight edits; eventual consolidation/reset separates momentary context from durable generalization. Evidence is reinforcement-learning environments and restricted theoretical assumptions, not personal autobiography, source attribution, privacy or language dialogue. Local nonparametric memory and lookup add storage/compute; periodic updates require lifecycle and rollback controls.

**A.L.I.C.E. already:** external/parametric/activation memory, MRM and governed model updates. **Action:** preserve as conditional candidate if a real temporary-state interference or continual-learning stability issue emerges; never let a temporary inferred preference override accepted Claim, subject identity, deletion/revocation or authorization. No training recipe change for native MFM formation.

## 8. FedAbA — heterogeneous-device adapters share parameter space, not necessarily a stable basis

**Primary:** Pengpeng Qiao et al., *Align Before Aggregation: Basis-Consistent Federated LoRA under Heterogeneous Ranks*, arXiv:2611.07598v1, November 5, https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07598v1

**Classification:** **Conditional future challenger** for multi-device/federated personal-model evolution, not an argument for changing selected transport or training.

**Method:** For unequal client LoRA ranks, align local update bases to a shared reference, reconstruct coefficients, weight by client-sample count and basis consistency, then apply SVD refactorization plus deterministic gauge to dispatch stable lower-rank prefixes. Theoretical results depend on linearized/idealized conditions. NLU/NLG and instruction-task experiments compare against HetLoRA, FLoRA, ECLoRA and other aggregators; for strongest reported rank heterogeneity NLU mean **82.90** vs ECLoRA **82.20** and Fed-PLoRA **82.10**; when homogeneous advantage is small (**80.97** vs next **80.80**). Additional CPU QR/SVD, floating-point and communication costs; assumes coordinated server/client learning with common base/authorized datasets.

**A.L.I.C.E. already:** multi-device federation semantics, signed model lineage, owner-authorized private compute and PyTorch/Accelerate adaptive placement. **New:** concrete potential parameter-basis mismatch when authorized independent devices later train different-rank personal adapters. **Action:** only test if Fable actually supports same-owner federated adapter aggregation across heterogeneous ranks and measured drift/accuracy becomes a blocker. Require compatible base/model generations, per-device source permissions, correction/deletion influence tracking, rollback, no shared unauthorized personal updates. No current federation stack change.
