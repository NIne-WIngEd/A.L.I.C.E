## 1. FGSE — when an experience-derived behavioral memory deserves promotion

**Primary:** *When Should Agents Remember? Falsification-Gated Self-Evolution for LLM Agents*, arXiv:2611.14326v1, **submitted November 10, 2026**, https://arxiv.org/html/2611.14326v1 . **Classification: future challenger to procedural learning, Mission/skill update admission and rollback; direct candidate for a future demonstrated scope/lesson-promotion defect, not a current repair.**

**Method:** Each tentative lesson is a structured and provenance-anchored hypothesis `precondition → behavior change → expected effect → verifier`. Apply in isolated temporary working state; check target-task transfer, deliberately constructed falsification counterexamples, and archived previously successful cases. A bounded multi-factor gate commits, refines or rejects. Post-commit monitoring can retire regressions. This is not a new canonical evidence system or authority model.

**Evidence:** Authors report WebArena 39.8% success, AppWorld average completion 78.8%, tau-Retail/Airline Pass@1 0.724/0.487 in their stated model configurations. Full system falsification failure 5.0%, archived regression damage 1.3%, harmful committed updates 3.6%. Removing falsification tests increases their reported falsification failure to 18.6% and harmful commits to 10.6%; removing archived regression tests raises old-capability damage to 5.2%. Without structured hypotheses WebArena performance is lower (35.6 vs 39.8). Results come from a preprint, heterogeneous interactive tasks and task-specific verifiers; some intervention metrics are verifier-dependent, not independent real-world safety guarantees.

**A.L.I.C.E. coverage / genuine delta:** The Experience Ledger, governed skill/mission memories, owner permissions, source evidence, lifecycle and versioned model/skill rollback already support candidate admission and correction. This paper supplies a **concrete falsifiable skill-hypothesis gate** with transfer + counterexample + old-competence checks, stronger than approving a lesson on one apparent success. Do not require every personal preference or source Claim to pass a software-style action verifier.

**Governance, upside, costs, action:** Only an authorized source/owner can authorize a procedure; a hypothesis is derived and cannot overwrite XTDB Claims or evidence. Preserve host/source-person/self boundaries, explicit scope/preconditions, revocation/deletion and model/data influence lineage. Benefits could be fewer overgeneralized procedures and reduced regression; costs include extra sandbox executions, probe generation and unreliable verifiers for real-world outcomes. **Future only:** when a *real* reusable procedure fails through scope overreach, test that one change against one adversarial precondition and one archived success before promotion, then retain rollback receipts. No broad validation harness or MFM change.

## 2. PMR — per-person learning of memory-update value

**Primary:** *Personalized Memory Rubrics: Learning What to Remember per User via Rubric-Guided Credit Assignment*, arXiv:2611.14017v1, **submitted November 10, 2026**, https://arxiv.org/html/2611.14017v1 . **Classification: future challenger for MFM write/update policy training, MRM and personal memory judgment.**

**Method:** Instead of redistributing terminal QA reward equally over a trajectory, derive a universal rubric from cross-persona failure patterns and a per-user residual rubric; teacher endorses intermediate write/edit choices at token level. Direction and entropy gates constrain rubric weight; successful trajectories get extra credit, failed ones fall back toward ordinary policy optimization. Co-evolved rubrics use quality/anti-circularity gates; inference requires **no extra rubric/judge call**, but learning incurs judge/teacher and RL training cost.

**Evidence:** On PersonaMem 32k reported memory-answer accuracy 76.76% vs listed MemCoE 67.47%; PersonaMem 128k 69.45% vs 58.67%; PrefEval explicit 87.6% vs 83.4%. Within-method 32k ablations: without positive-only learning 57.23%, without rubric strength control 62.16%, without direction gate 67.03%, without entropy gate 65.66%. Without cross-persona sharing 54.05% vs complete 76.76%; global-only and per-user-only are also weaker than their combination. The training uses Qwen2.5-7B, eight A100 GPUs, repeated sampled trajectories/rubric updates. Some listed comparator systems employ different model sizes. Evaluations are mostly memory-question performance; token-level rubric endorsement is not a causal proof of memory usefulness (compare previously tracked LEDGER).

**A.L.I.C.E. coverage / new delta:** Native memory formation plus per-host MRM policy, provenance-aware learning and experience outcomes are already required. The new candidate is **host-conditioned learning credit** on memory formation decisions rather than global one-size-fits-all retention rules. User-local data isolation is crucial: the paper's cross-persona sharing success is NOT permission to leak individual memory data, rubric deltas, private examples or learned weights across Fable instances.

**Governance, upside, costs, action:** Rubric preference cannot grant Claim Authority, overwrite corrected facts, confer permission, blur person identity or override deletion. Source-backed gold proposals and held-out native formation remain the actual criterion. Upside could be better per-host write-selection generalization at zero online judge overhead; risks are circular reward shaping, privacy leakage, increased RL data/compute and evaluation overfit. **Future only:** consider for one measured per-host memory-update training deficiency **after** checkpoint-26/native MFM formation is qualified. Do not change the ongoing training recipe.

## 3. MIRIX-2.0 — n-ary provenance frames and bounded graph-supported evidence

**Primary:** *MIRIX-2.0: Multi-Agent Memory System*, arXiv:2611.12068v1, **submitted November 9, 2026**, https://arxiv.org/html/2611.12068v1 . **Classification: already covered/corroboration with a specific future derived-graph recollection candidate.**

**Method:** Six specialized typed writers store full memory records. A derived, citation-linked **n-ary hypergraph** represents people, events, participants/roles, time and literal quantities. Writes commit to records first, graph indexing is best effort, and failed graph queries fall back to evidence search. A bounded query planner retrieves graph paths and the supporting original records. Offline AutoDream merges identity-stable anchors without rewriting events/records and distills procedural skills.

**Evidence/ablation:** LoCoMo complete model 91.4% vs flat-record variant 85.7%, graph/no-dream 88.4%; LongMemEval-S 72.6% vs 58.8% flat and 70.0% graph/no-dream. ALFWorld 76.4±6.2% vs compared AWM 73.6±1.9%; WebShop 37.4±2.1% vs 36.0±4.3%, uncertain small difference. Offline maintenance/write overhead is nontrivial; 6-agent ingestion can take substantially longer than flat; timed retrieval comparisons often disable AutoDream, so reported speed is not full online+offline cost. Benchmarks are mainly QA and synthetic tool worlds. There is no demonstrated cross-layer deletion, authority adjudication, or host-level continual model learning.

**A.L.I.C.E. coverage / new delta:** Cognitive Multi-Graph, engine-independent associative graph compute, Episodes, temporal/causal relations and source-native evidence are already mandated. Novel useful implementation pattern is *role-qualified n-ary derived frames*, evidence-first write plus fallback and bounded citation-backed traversal. **Do not copy MIRIX's vector record plane as canonical truth**; KurrentDB events and XTDB Claims stay canonical. A graph merge cannot merge distinct real people, an event must retain separate IDs, and all derived anchors require correction/deletion rebuild lineage.

**Action:** If real group/person/time recollection fails on which participant did what when, test one role-qualified derived frame backed by actual evidence and current authority. Preserve index rebuild and rollback. No graph engine change, memory-plane count ceiling or infrastructure tournament.

## 4. CAMP-II — trained memory operations through familiar file tools

**Primary:** *Coding Agent Memory Post-Training II: Unlocking the Memory Potential of Pre-trained File Operations for Long-Horizon Tasks via On-Policy Distillation*, arXiv:2611.14009v1, **submitted November 10, 2026**, https://arxiv.org/html/2611.14009v1 . **Classification: future challenger for Cognitive Workspace, memory-control policy training, long Mission context resets.**

**Method:** In five long-horizon environments (Shop, Coding, AutoResearch, General, Visual), both teacher and student can create/edit/read/search ordinary workspace files across context replacements using Bash, an interface pretraining already taught. Fully asynchronous on-policy distillation trains a 4B/9B agent against frozen large teacher with importance/clipping, including supervision of memory-write tokens. Their comparison isolates the difficulty of unfamiliar bespoke memory tools and compaction protocols.

**Evidence:** Five-domain mean for 4B CAMP-II 64.6% vs prior CAMP-I 54.2% and initial 4B 45.3%; five seeds for full method about 64.9±0.5. Familiar file interface vs substituted dedicated memory operations yields roughly 16–17-point difference in controlled comparisons; masking memory-write-token learning substantially hurts. Transfers to Terminal-Bench 2.1 30.0% vs 23.1% baseline for 4B, SWE-Bench Pro V2 18.1% vs 11.2%. Requires teacher, substantial RL/OPD and long trajectories, and only evaluates coding/task success. Does not guarantee correct personal-memory formation, Claim Authority, data deletion or success of the present MFM recipe.

**A.L.I.C.E. coverage / new delta/action:** Workspace/activation memory, source-native file tools, MRM and cross-session durability are already present in the destination. The paper shows a trainable ***interface-prior*** effect. A working file directory may be a temporary training/execution affordance, never canonical evidence or permission. Persisted files must be scoped, source-cited and deletion-aware. **Future:** revisit only if real model memory-tool operation suffers from unfamiliar interface and a control with familiar tools changes the next training decision. Do not swap current MFM objective or replace governed event and Claim planes with files.

## 5. Agentic-OPD — recovering checkpoint capabilities lost through training

**Primary:** *Agentic-OPD: Recovering Forgotten Agent Capabilities via On-Policy Distillation for Agentic Tasks*, arXiv:2611.12799v1, **submitted November 9, 2026**, https://arxiv.org/html/2611.12799v1 . **Classification: future personal-model evolution/rollback challenger.**

**Method:** Earlier RL checkpoints form frozen teachers. Highest Coverage Next (HCN) chooses a teacher complementary to the present student's failures; Locality-Aware Distillation (LAD) filters to cases and turns where that teacher is better. The goal is one current student retaining more of the union of previous capabilities without overwriting newly learned strengths.

**Evidence:** Up to +7.4 percentage points over initial best-validation checkpoint in deep search; controlled 3B NQ 42.1→45.4, Hotpot 34.8→37.9, dropping either HCN or LAD reduces gains. But one tracked subset gained 60 newly solved questions **while losing 45 previously solved** (net +15 of 2,000): not monotonic capability retention or proof of safe evolution. Requires historical checkpoints, extra on-policy sampling and multi-GPU training.

**A.L.I.C.E. coverage / new delta/action:** Fable already requires content-addressed model lineage, governed evolution, regression checks and rollback. Paper adds a plausible way to mine complementary prior generations when a concrete regression appears. **Do not distill from a revoked/deleted or disallowed training generation; archive access follows model lineage and deletion gates.** Future only after native learnable capability exists and a real successor regression is measured; test targeted recovered behavior plus old capability and influence echo, not exhaustive checkpoint tournaments.

## 6. Spectral Unlearning — optimizer geometry for parametric correction

**Primary:** *Spectral Unlearning: Transformer Structure-Preserving Updates for Language Models*, arXiv:2611.11902v1, **submitted November 9, 2026**, https://arxiv.org/html/2611.11902v1 . **Classification: future parametric-memory correction/unlearning challenger.**

**Method:** Instead of assuming scalar-independent parameter updates, apply matrix-structured spectral/preconditioned steps to standard GD, NPO or RMU unlearning objectives; motivates direction through transformer normalized linear layers. Tested with Llama-3-family and Zephyr on TOFU, RWKU and WMDP (varied forgetting, retain and utility measures), generally improving aggregate forgetting/retain tradeoff against comparable scalar optimizers and approaching retain-trained references in several settings. Results are method- and metric-dependent. Additional optimizer state, eigen/matrix operations and multi-GPU training have compute/memory cost; certain output facts can be recovered by prompting or later training and benchmarks cannot prove erasure.

**A.L.I.C.E. coverage / new delta/action:** Cross-layer deletion/unlearning, model weights, dataset lineage, shadow activation, external stores, projection invalidation and influence-echo tests are already required. This is a **candidate optimizer**, not a replacement for deletion coordinator, retention audit, rollback or proof of private knowledge removal. Examine only if an actual selected personal adapter has a measured post-deletion retain/forget failure that the optimizer might fix. No change today.

## 7. Unprompted agent coordination through shared writable state

**Primary:** *Unprompted coordination through shared memory in LLM agents: a locked two-implementation replication and extension*, arXiv:2611.12412v1, **submitted November 9, 2026**, https://arxiv.org/html/2611.12412v1 . **Classification: already covered/corroboration, conditional shared L1/Valkey and multi-device governance check.**

**Method and evidence:** Four LLM agents each solve a distributed arithmetic piece with a scratch key-value interface described as their private workspace but actually shared. With identical private stores, joint success disappears (reported 49/50 and 47/50 shared worlds versus 0/50 private). A routine instruction to recheck workspace changes one tested model's cross-agent reading from 1/50 to 50/50 on both independently locked implementations. The study separates writes, reads and actual influence, and warns that generic key collisions can appear to be intentional communication. It does not establish malicious collusion intent or real-world breach rates; the environment is deliberately minimal.

**A.L.I.C.E. coverage / new delta/action:** Shared ephemeral memory, multi-device federation and multi-agent Missions already require tenant/host/agent namespaces, per-object authorization, provenance and audit. The new replicated observation is that **shared writable state is an implicit communication channel even without an explicit messaging tool**. Store visibility does not grant right to read, persist or promote another agent's information. If a real L1/Valkey/shared scratch execution path begins exchanging data across unauthorized subjects, validate one cross-namespace read/write denial and trace the source; don't add general cross-agent benchmark or new transport.
