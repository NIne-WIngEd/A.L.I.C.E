## 1. From Acting to Learning — gradient-aware selection of successful experience for training

**Primary:** Yan et al., *From Acting to Learning: Experience Selection toward Continually Learning Agents*, **arXiv:2611.27159v1**, submitted November 20. https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27159 and https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.27159

**Classification:** **future challenger** for FBM training-data selection and downstream Mission/procedural learning, **not** a current MFM fix.

**Method/assumptions:** Collect a pool of already successful action records. Compare nine record selectors under a fixed Qwen-family ~4B actor, shared training recipe and executor. Global gradient rule scores candidate training examples based on optimizer-aware effects using LoRA gradient and saved Adam-style states; comparators include random selection, high/low NLL, task/action quota matching and gradient ranking. Control selection freedom: same 256 unique contents and 768 presentations across the global selection rules. The method assumes actions may be replayed/graded, source rewards are credible, and the training objective is informative about later agent performance.

**Experiments and ablations:** Two ALFWorld experience pools (API and generic), 8 weight-initialization seeds, 2 sampling seeds, 288 trained students in the complete development grid; each tested on 128 task groups under common executor. Global gradient selection reaches **81.445% API, 73.193% generic**, versus global random **73.193/67.236%** and global high-NLL **77.344/69.141%**; improvements +8.252/+5.957 points over random and +4.102/+4.052 over high NLL. All eight initialization-level contrasts positive against both baselines. Independently frozen 128-task reserved replication reports +7.03 points versus random and +5.03 versus high NLL. Crucial ablation: relaxing task/action coverage quotas improves gradient ranking but hurts random ranking, hence a free selection space is *not* automatically beneficial.

**Limits/cost/evidence:** One learning round, controlled textual environment, fixed 4B student and task pools. This is **not** proof of lifelong continual learning, temporal/causal personal experience formation, host identity development, or resistance to catastrophic forgetting; equal record count/presentations does not prove equal token/gradient-computation cost. Gradient scoring is expensive and requires gradient/optimizer access, so not online-by-default for private users. Evidence **moderate for supervised action-selection effectiveness within the tested ALFWorld regime**, low for general personal learning.

**Already vs new/impact:** FBM already owns labeled/source-authorized teaching, model/data lineage, trainer optimization and independent eval; Experience Ledger already records actions and outcomes. New candidate: select **training-informative** successful records using optimizer-aware marginal effects rather than assuming every successful action teaches equally or highest NLL is best. **Future-only** impact on FBM procedural curriculum; no completed/current construction contract requires a change.

**Governance/action:** Train only on authorized source/outcome trajectories. Preserve original Experience and Claim authority, host/source-person/assistant-self/relationship scopes, source and teacher lineage, revoked-data exclusion, adapter generations and rollback; a selector score cannot validate an event or authorize an action. Only if an existing FBM/skill training lane exhibits a specific plateau with redundant successful traces, compare one bounded selection intervention on that real task family before adopting. No new campaign now.

## 2. VMSA — procedural retrieval skill induction over complementary perceptual memories

**Primary:** Choi et al., *VMSA: Video Memory Skill Agent for Long Video Understanding*, **arXiv:2611.27381v1**, submitted November 20. https://arxiv.org/html/2611.27381v1

**Classification:** **future challenger** for multimodal Recollection Orchestrator, perceptual history and reusable evidence-search procedures; stronger controlled skill-specific follow-up to existing EvoMem, not new storage plane.

**Method/assumptions:** Generate time-bounded fine-grained segment captions; consolidate VLM-verified per-entity temporal events from mutual-kNN candidate profile groups; compose entity-aware long-range episodes and a global summary. At query time expose episode context and let a controller search caption records, per-entity events, and when enabled original video for confirmation. Separately induce procedural retrieval skills from **successful source trajectories on disjoint task groups**; for each trajectory decide skip/refine/add skill, consolidate after every 100, retrieve top-3, up to ten search turns. The tested configuration uses Qwen3.5-9B for memory construction, Qwen3.6-27B as acting controller, GPT-5.6 for skill induction, and reused precomputed WorldMM captions for week-long videos.

**Experiments/ablations:** Seven multiple-choice long-video QA datasets. Week-long **Ego-R1 74.7% versus WorldMM 65.3%** (+9.4 points), EgoLifeQA **66.8 versus 65.6%**, EgoMemReason **38.6%** (best compared 37.4%, average question spans 5.1 evidence segments). Removing captions lowers EgoLifeQA **66.8→57.6**, removing entity-events **66.8→63.6**, removing episode context **66.8→62.4**. Without induced skills Ego-R1 **70.7→74.7** with skills, temporal ground-truth overlap (tIoG) **0.597→0.641**; LongVideoBench **71.3→74.1** for no-skills versus complete method. Comparison produces 15 induced skills versus hundreds/thousands for some compared methods and stronger results on two benchmarks.

**Limits/cost/evidence:** Week-long experiments **disable source-video Segment Inspection** to save cost, and reuse released caption outputs, so not proof of always verifying raw video. Multiple-choice factual QA not continuous private identity, year-long video, consent/permission, causal personal judgment. Construction needs long-video VLM inference plus cross-window entity clustering; incremental skills and 27B controller require nontrivial inference/API cost. Duplicate/incorrect entity merges could smear observer identity, time, or source-person; captions and episodes remain fallible derived views. Evidence **moderate for layered retrieval and learned search skill transfer**, low for fully sovereign lifelong perceptual cognition.

**Already vs new/impact:** A.L.I.C.E. already has original encrypted multimodal evidence, distinct person scopes, temporal events/episodes, graph+Qdrant, source-native verification and procedural learning. New **joint** mechanism is teaching evidence-source selection, verification and stopping *as transferable procedures* from verified retrieval outcomes, beyond merely adding more memory or similarity search. Future-only; not grounds to select a three-level permanent memory ontology or a new video backend.

**Governance/action:** Every entity/episode/skill must carry original time interval, observed vs inferred role, subject identity, derivation dependencies, rights/version, revoke and rebuild status. Episode context is projection, never Claims; source-native inspection remains possible according to permissions regardless of paper's cost ablation. If a real video-history task retrieves the wrong observer/object occurrence or searches repeatedly, test a focused one-skill retrieval-policy adaptation against existing EvoMem-like behavior and original evidence. No current change.

## 3. Microfitting — reusable source-specific parametric adapters with bounded feedback-driven training data

**Primary:** Ryan et al., *Microfitting: Continual Learning via Cached Test-Time Training*, **arXiv:2611.20258v1**, submitted November 15, surfaced in today's broader private-scaling scan. https://arxiv.org/html/2611.20258v1

**Classification:** **future challenger** for MRM's optional source-conditioned parametric memory and FBM dataset recipe evolution, **not** core Claim storage replacement.

**Method/assumptions:** Freeze general DeepSeek V4 Flash (large model); train and cache a small **per-document adapter**, initialized through a hypernetwork, on generated QA targeted at observed base-model correctable failures; apply matching source adapter at answering along with the underlying document text. Outer-loop controller updates question-synthesis recipes based on fresh held-out adapter outcomes, accepting revisions only when they outperform incumbent on paired fresh data. Parameter isolation avoids cross-document weight overwrite but creates a growing adapter inventory. Training-generated knowledge does not supplant document evidence.

**Experiments/ablations:** Enterprise Document Operations three fresh rounds, **10 documents/200 questions per round**, reported largest fresh-batch relative improvement **38.2%** over base; same-batch recipe comparison supports some contribution beyond document changes. Separate correctable-error study shifts held-out target distribution across recipe versions and reports .399 base vs .621 microfit at final targeted stage (**55.6% relative**), **NOT** an apples-to-apples fixed-workload trend. On same 204 questions, 12/24/40/85 training questions per document produce **.632/.696/.735/.725** accuracy, while cost rises. Batch comparison of only two documents is not broad capacity evidence. Optimized two-RTX-PRO-6000 workstation using RAM offload and shared-prefix/cache reduces a fixed 50-step run from ~112 minutes to **16.8 minutes** (~6.7×) versus its own naive deployment.

**Limits/cost/evidence:** Realistic but small enterprise domain and **2.2k–4k token** docs; only three rounds, one seed in main series, target-question generator static, no measured drift in live user query distribution; LLM-judged short answers. Two large workstation GPUs and model offload, many per-document adapters and synthetic question traces create meaningful compute, RAM, disk, and privacy overhead. Evidence **moderate for targeted enterprise-document adaptation with isolated parameters**, low for general life history, personal self/relationship, or on-device laptop feasibility.

**Already vs new/impact:** Fable already has exact/source-native retrieval, MRM scheduling across parametric/external/activation, model/dataset lineage and FBM governed evolution. Novel optional optimization: source-conditioned *offline* specialized adapters + learning to generate only examples correcting observed model weaknesses, rather than making all document memory parametric. Future-only, not evidence that Fable should use one adapter per file or train 284B backbone locally.

**Governance/action:** Keep original docs/Claim Authority canonical. Adapters, teacher questions, caches, retained snapshots and model lineage must be keyed to specific authorized source versions, subjects, consent and deletion scopes; correction/revocation disables or rebuilds affected adapters and tests for echoes. Never silently merge private-source adapters into shared Fable builder weights. Only examine if a measured analytical/source QA failure persists despite correct exact evidence and model training is actually justified by latency/accuracy. No current task.

## 4. MoLR — token-dependent recursive low-rank adaptation

**Primary:** Luo et al., *Mixture of Latent Recursions for Low-Rank Adaptation*, **arXiv:2611.27823v1**, submitted November 20. https://arxiv.org/html/2611.27823v1

**Classification:** **future challenger** for efficient private model/adapters and training/serving mechanisms, not source-grounded formation fix.

**Method:** Shared latent update recursively refines LoRA's down-projected per-token state with a threshold router controlling depth, balancing token use across depth and permitting test-time depth changes without retraining. A dynamic nonmergeable latent computation mechanism, not an external memory or native self model.

**Evidence/ablation:** Llama-3.2-3B NLR average **59.21 LoRA→61.21 MoLR** across ARC-C/BoolQ/GSM8K/HellaSwag/HumanEval/MMLU/WinoGrande; Qwen3-8B **75.28→76.41**. Controlled identical backbone ablation on Llama3.2-3B: no recursion **59.21**, fixed **60.46**, random **59.53**, input-dependent **61.21**, supporting token-specific selection, not depth alone. Image classification improvements also reported. Computational table per training step **0.55→0.62 s** (~13%) and peak GPU **20.76→21.12 GiB** (<2%); per-token inference **0.18→0.69 ms** vs ordinary mergeable LoRA, making latency tradeoff real. Benchmark-specific hardware/results do not establish performance of source-bound MFM or native personal judgment.

**Already/new/impact:** PyTorch/Accelerate hardware-adaptive training and governed adapters already required. New conditional way to spend more *per-token inference compute* without a proportionate parameter count, not a new cognitive plane. **Future-only**; no current optimizer change, and no fixed rank/depth/model ceiling.

**Governance/action:** All routed model generations and cache states remain versioned, permission-bound, reversible and tested against revoked-source echoes. Only test if a working personal adapter faces measured fidelity-versus-weight/VRAM tradeoff and can afford slower decode. Explicitly **not** a restart of MFM 0/9 source-alignment work.

## 5. CurvDelta / CurvLoRA — preservation-constrained learning and forgetting

**Primary:** *CurvDelta: Low-Curvature Delta Modeling For Surgical Learning and Unlearning*, **arXiv:2611.24654v1**, submitted November 18, newly surfaced in deletion/continual-training scan. https://arxiv.org/html/2611.24654v1

**Classification:** **future challenger** for governed personal-model retention/unlearning optimizer; not verified deletion or architecture invalidation.

**Method/assumptions:** Estimate locally quadratic preservation-set KL loss curvature around base weights using block-diagonal K-FAC; construct low-curvature two-sided spectral projection for realized model delta, either dense or projected LoRA. Contrasts with simply projecting gradients each step: projected parameters keep the actual applied adaptive-optimizer update in the selected subspace. Preservation quality depends on representative retain data and local quadratic approximation.

**Evidence/ablations:** On TOFU 5% forget (Llama3.1-8B, SimNPO) CurvDelta forget-answer probability **6.3** vs retrained **10.8** and standard LoRA **22.6**, model utility **63.3** vs retrained **63.2**. In TOFU 10% forget, CurvLoRA forget probability **3.4** and utility **62.5**; CurvDelta **6.5** and **63.2**, retrained **10.6** and **64.6**. On MUSE Books CurvDelta verbatim memorization **11.6** vs retrain **14.2** but retention **55.8** vs retrain **68.6**, so **not** universally best retention or deletion. Math/code tasks show better acquisition/retention trade-off in selected backbones. Dimension-matched low-curvature, high-curvature, random-projection ablations favor low-curvature; delta-parameterization beats gradient projection for tested unlearning trade-off. Curvature estimation adds upfront K-FAC data/compute and cached eigenspaces; reuse only if same base/preservation set and allowed lineage. Evidence **moderate for controlled optimization tradeoffs, low for private information secure erasure**.

**Already/new/impact:** Existing correction/deletion/unlearning, adaptive adapter training, preservation suites and rollback require capability retention. Novel optimization technique is **training within a retain-loss-defined geometry**, separate from November 19 tracked synaptic partitioning and November 21 lifespan decay. Future-only; not grounds to declare unlearning complete.

**Governance/action:** Never equate benchmark forgetting with removal of source influence, weight genealogy or decrypted data. All adapters, parent checkpoints, caches, generations and synthetic descendants require explicit revocation/rebuild, retained capability tests, re-extraction/attack probes and owner gates. Consider one controlled implementation comparison only after an actual adapter fails target forgetting or retention with existing selected method; do not alter current MFM/N0 recipes.
