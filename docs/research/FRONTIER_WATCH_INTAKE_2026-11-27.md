## 1. Under the Influence – of What? — model attribution is not evidence custody

Primary: Sindlinger et al., https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34373v1 (25 Nov; EACL 2027 accepted per arXiv listing). Already covered/corroboration with future diagnostic value for parametric lineage and deletion. Full paper now available after November 26 screening.

Method: matched full versus filtered corpus training from scratch, confirm target property acquired only by full model, compare influence rankings on contrastive correct/incorrect probes. GradSim, TrackStar and K-FAC via Bergson versus BM25. Four linguistic phenomena and BEAR factual relations; English Common Corpus and Wikipedia; attribution over 5M sampled sentences plus fact-specific material, not entire corpora.
Evidence: best precision@10 on direct factual co-occurrence only 35% Common Corpus and 26% Wikipedia. For Wikipedia K-FAC, 66% of retrieved examples mention only one relevant entity, 19% co-mention the pair. Partial linguistic cues also rank highly; K-FAC generally strongest, TrackStar stronger for facts. Filtering reduced full-corpus-supported fact accuracy only 3.7/3.2 points; evaluation restricted to facts that changed under intervention. Single training seed, imperfect filtering, sampling, small GPT-2-type models, no secure unlearning test. Reproducing reported experiments estimated 730 GPU-hours (1,730 total including failed attempts).
Already/new: Fable already requires dataset ancestry, influence lineage, deletion and rollback; new controlled evidence quantifies how poorly approximate attribution can recover direct factual support even where training-data intervention affects learning. A score is neither a proof of influence absence nor a source assertion.
Action: if diagnosing a specific parametric influence echo, use attribution only as a prioritization aid alongside exact lineage and clean retraining/retirement where needed. Do not narrow revocation to top-k attributed examples, treat association as a Claim, or re-teach from generated traces. Keep subject boundaries and model generations. No current attribution campaign or deletion migration. This is not evidence that the existing MFM optimizer attribution is wrong; that measures the actual objective/moments, not pretraining-example provenance.

## 2. Learning from Unreliable Operator References — verify what the learner is allowed and able to know

Primary: Stoianov et al., https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34341v1 (25 Nov). Future challenger, FBM teaching eligibility and evidence-grounded judgment.
Method: offline Walker decomposes historical operator replies and checks available knowledge-base and tool evidence; human editors repair missing access/docs; verified claim coverage plus precision against evidence actually retrieved in the learner's trajectory, explicit rule/intent checks and GRPO; read-only tools; typed answer/clarify/handoff outcomes.
Evidence: 74.8% of 8,680 claims from 2,760 replies verified; only 54.8% of replies had no unresolved claims/questions. On offline text answers SFT hallucination 13.6%, final GRPO 8.6%; intent match falls 80.4% to 71.5% while answer rate rises 60.2% to 88.9%. Gold KB substitution on 207 KB-dependent requests changes final GRPO 8.3% to 8.2% hallucination. With F1 reward, verified-only coverage versus all claims reduces hallucination 39.1% to 32.5%; coverage-only is much worse. Online A/B automation 63.3% to 80.8%, closure not necessarily resolution. Human production assessment unsupported facts 5.0% SFT vs 0.6% final GRPO (800 conversations/model). Single organization, language, referral domain, 27B model, proprietary records, judge-sensitive text-only metric excludes assumptions in clarifying questions. No write actions or identity-learning test. Additional claim verification, expert review, trajectory sampling and RL compute; no verified GPU-hour or personal-device result.
Already/new: FBM-01/02/04, MFM source-first admission and independent authority already require this separation. New empirically grounded design for coverage targets limited to demonstrator claims that are supported by information actually available to the student, and precision scored against the student's own opened evidence. Missing source access is not a reason to label unsupported claims true or delete legitimate teaching objectives.
Action: if actual future FBM/EIPM supervision includes privileged teacher information, diagnose the missing evidence/permissions before revising the target; compare only affected evidence-grounded outcomes and retain abstention. Never relax identity, disclosure, Claim or deletion boundaries; separate author/learner evidence and synthetic training data. No change to existing MFM target, objective or checkpoint26 plan.

## 3. CARE-OPD — replay consequential turns, including recovery and verification

Primary: Hou et al., https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34756v1 (25 Nov). Future challenger, FBM procedural/model evolution efficiency.
Method: mix verified teacher trajectories with student-seeded prefixes, reject low-confidence student-response distillation samples, use downstream outcomes to select replay turns rather than position decay; generate fresh student responses from stored prefixes, distill teacher token distributions without executing those new responses. Four GPU nodes, 60 updates, 3 seeds; two Qwen families, SWE and research tasks.
Evidence: SWE Qwen3.5 59.5% vs async one-turn OPD 57.1%, 3.0 vs 5.5h and 171.2k vs 308.1k environment actions, all stages counted. Vanilla replay 53.7% at 2.9h; matched 40% random turns 53.7%, hindsight 57.3%. Student-seeded plus confidence gate 57.1% vs teacher-only/no gate 53.7%. BrowseComp-Plus 49.6% vs 47.2% full online OPD; Qwen3 SWE 23.0 vs 20.3% one-turn online OPD. Confidence is not correctness; frozen student roll-ins become stale, new replay outputs not executed. Not a laptop qualification; retains trajectories and repeated teacher/student inference.
Already/new: FBM retains traces, failed/repair outcomes and governed training eligibility. Prior AutoMaster and gradient-selection work does not establish this particular learner-conditioned history coverage plus hindsight replay allocation. New practical means of limiting repeated expensive environment execution without losing late critical decisions.
Action: future-only if actual FBM procedural distillation is bottlenecked by environment replay. Preserve raw outcomes, policy/version and permissions; separate counterfactual/unexecuted responses from Experience; drop revoked replay prefixes and derivatives, version/rollback weights, independently qualify native behavior. No MFM changes or replay campaign.

## 4. SPLICE — local repairs selected by the learner's actual ability to continue

Primary: Hou et al., https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34777v1 (25 Nov). Future challenger, FBM/Mission procedural learning. Related authors to CARE-OPD but distinct algorithm and setting.
Method: in binary-reward all-fail groups (no relative reward gradient), propose bounded options at a branchable state in a failed student trajectory, evaluate each with current-policy continuations (three by default), use positive returns to weight local supervision. Other groups use normal GRPO. Unlike full teacher solutions, the learner's own ability to finish determines option usefulness.
Evidence: gains over GRPO 2.8–9.8 points across four SWE/research settings; initial-failure graduation 18.9% vs 8.1% GRPO and 15.9% ReLIFT. Qwen3 SWE ablation resolve 25.7% vs 21.2% single-option, 24.3% hard best, 23.7% uniform positive; teacher continuations 26.2% but 372.5k proposal turns vs 93.5k with current-policy continuation. More/longer guidance not always better. Requires branching environments, repeated rollouts and trustworthy outcome verifier; three samples noisy; increases cost over ordinary GRPO. No native formation or personal-model judgment experiment.
Already/new: FRESH repair graphs and FBM diagnosis preserve failure/repair alternatives. New return-weighted learning of bounded repairs based on actual current learner continuations, rather than copying teacher success. MFM's checkpoint10 failure is supervised complete-target learning, not a GRPO all-fail group; analogy alone is not evidence of a vanishing reward gradient.
Action: future-only for a demonstrated all-fail procedural RL bottleneck. Use sandboxed authorized replays and preserve actual versus simulated outcomes, identity scope, provenance and deletion lineage; keep complete consumer evaluation and reversible model generations. No splicing expected MFM output, no relaxed target or new training authorization.
