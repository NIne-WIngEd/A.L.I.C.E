## 1. World-model uncertainty can disappear before the estimator sees it

Primary: Baur et al., https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34830v1 (25 Nov). Future challenger: perceptual/world model, epistemic calibration, Mission planning.

Method: train LeWorldModel (JEPA) and DreamerV3 (RSSM) on matched observations in ten simulated control environments. Freeze models. Perturb physical state at one or multiple frames and trace relative sensitivity from renderer through encoder, posterior/recurrent state, predictive feature and last-layer Laplace uncertainty. Five-head ensemble is a control. Evaluate withheld regions, error ranking, violations, distribution shifts, and CEM planning with uncertainty cost.

Evidence: single-frame sensitivity near 1 at both encoders and Dreamer posterior, then ~0.2 after Dreamer's GRU; LeWM predictor ~0.9. Last-layer uncertainty inherits lost signal. In withheld-region tests LeWM wins 13/17 body/object cells; Dreamer wins some (not universal). Example: Cube double object's AUROC .97 vs .67. Cube teleport AUROC .51 prior, .84 posterior, .90 LeWM; posterior can inspect the actual current frame but cannot substitute for prior in imagined planning. Estimator ensemble agrees closely (r>=.98 across task scores). Persistent shifts may restore recurrent sensitivity; low reconstruction-gradient share for small objects is associated with continuing attenuation. Planning benefit 17–28 percentage points in four environments, not universal; multi-object LeWM also fails some tests. One instance per architecture, simulator intervention, last-layer estimators only, no controlled retraining without reconstruction term or altered recurrent gate. No proof that all recurrent models are deficient.

Costs: Laplace Gram/covariance O(F^2) storage and per-query compute, fit O(NF^2+F^3); ensemble requires additional training and heads. Paper compares costs on H200 and CPU, not personal-device full workload. Existing raw observations and model feature tracing would be needed for a focused diagnostic; no general retraining/architecture change.

Already/new: Fable already separates live observations, historical Experience, predictive world/causal state, uncertainty, and verified Mission outcomes. New diagnostic locates where a relevant observation disappears before an uncertainty estimator can react. Confidence in a derived representation does not certify that the original evidence was preserved. Distinguish current observation novelty from uncertainty during hypothetical planning.

Action only if actual perceptual or world-model behavior misses a brief or low-salience but consequential change: hold the source constant, perturb the relevant factor in a controlled case, trace features, compare observation-conditioned and prediction-conditioned uncertainty if applicable. If signal is lost upstream, do not merely tune a downstream confidence threshold. Keep hypothetical interventions separate from actual Experience and scope uncertainty to source/subject/time; preserve revocation of derived features and estimator ancestry, version/rollback. Do not select JEPA or replace N0 on this evidence; no current MFM repair.

## 2. INSPIRE — known-person priors versus newly accumulated individual evidence

Primary: Zhang et al., https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34708v1 (25 Nov). Future challenger: Cognitive Multi-Graph learned forecasting/ranking; host/source-person/world state cold-start calibration.

Method: two-stage temporal link forecasting over a known entity catalog, chronological score-then-reveal. Stage I uses gated event-updated memory read across query AND candidate endpoints, training-only history masking. Stage II freezes predictor; learns semantic prior and bounded history residual with correlation-weighted evidence and role-balanced supervision. Cholesky-weighted event aggregates cached per entity; training-only prior correction. Longitudinal cohorts tracked both by query and equal entity weight.

Evidence: relative Emerging MRR gain 16.73–88.83% vs strongest reported comparator; paired evidence adapter improves Answer-new MRR 7.81–41.24%. GDELT Answer-new .1541 frozen stage I to .2176 full; comparator TransFIR .0801. Candidate competition explains much of gain on two streams (72.8%, 69.9% under authors' decomposition), i.e. correcting over-ranked established distractors, not merely promoting newcomers. Correlation weighting and long-range history ablations are mixed; removing them improves some GDELT results. The Stage I masking benefit varies with stream regime. Both-new sample counts 86 and 2 on two datasets. Geopolitical forecasting benchmarks, known catalog, timestamped links rather than autobiographical facts, no open-world identity resolution/causal truth/deletion proof; some comparator results from earlier papers. Stage II training 2.17–8.24 GiB peak, 23–41% of Stage I time on ICEWS and 62% on GDELT. Queue storage scales with catalog size times K and full scoring with query batch times catalog size. K=32 is an experiment setting, not a Fable memory cap.

Already/new: existing cognitive multi-graph, bitemporal Claims, subject separation and individual evolution cover the logical requirements. New ranking diagnostic: sparse-evidence newcomer as query versus answer/candidate, and equal-person cohort weighting to expose dominance by frequent entities. Shared learned patterns should be explicitly separated from authorized individual evidence; stronger prediction is not proof of identity or a verified Claim.

Action only for a measured cold-start graph forecasting/recollection issue: compare affected endpoint roles and control established distractors under unchanged valid/knowledge-time visibility; inspect candidate-state calibration before training a residual. Rebuild/expire derived states on correction/revocation and retain original source identity, subject and lineage; do not infer new person attributes as fact or fix candidate universe. No change to LadybugDB/NebulaGraph, XTDB or graph stack. No new MFM/N0 training requirement.

## 3. Induced theories as reusable analytic memory: missing preconditions and false generalization

Primary: Gao & Bi, https://arxiv.org/html/2611.34751v1 (25 Nov). Future challenger: analytic memory, procedural learning, FBM, Recollection.

Method: length-matched oracle theory, irrelevant, generic hint and worked example contexts. Train/test probability problem generators, planted unpublished mathematical conditions and pre-exposure recall probes; independent solver scored numerically. Induction requires exploration with code in revised protocol; merger sees 12 partial notes and training problems only. Recovery ratio measures fraction of oracle gain on non-trap held-out cases; trap cases separately measure misapplication.

Evidence: Qwen3-14B 12k cap pass@1 .37 to .83 with oracle theory, median correct solution tokens 10.2k to 6.3k, truncation 59% to 14%; worked examples no comparable gain. At larger 64k cap on stronger solver classic-family gain .07. Planted-family strong induction recovers .60 of oracle gain on F1, .48 on F3 and ~0 on F2 (correct broad form but missing necessary condition). F3 wrong formula run .01 vs .37 no-theory. Best single note on classic family R=.47, learned merger .61, human union .84. F1 64k strong solver nearly all completed solutions correct; context helps finish under budget rather than proving novel reasoning skill. Three inductions per condition, one model family, synthetic probability tasks, poorly agreeing theory judges and textbook proof methods even in planted families. Compute substantial: follow-up experiments 128.2 GB200/GB300 node-hours, excludes initial experiments, H100 induction runs and judges. Not a personal-device or autonomous trustworthy theory-learning result.

Already/new: Fable already includes analytic memory, procedural learning and independently qualified FBM; prior AdaMM does not prove that an abstract rule's operational prerequisites survive consolidation. This paper adds a length-matched, counterexample-oriented distinction between recalled examples, genuinely induced reusable rules and rules missing an operative condition. Helps diagnose when a stored explanation sounds right but cannot actually guide the model. Correctness of a theory and its usefulness at a given serving-model budget are distinct.

Action only for a real repeated analytic/procedural inference bottleneck: derive candidate rule from authorized source/outcome evidence; compare against matched examples on held-out cases and counterexamples, explicitly retain conditions and exceptions, then measure net reuse cost. Version the derived hypothesis and maintain parent evidence/permissions; new synthesis does not become Claim Authority or observed Experience. Revoked source invalidates affected derivative/replay lineage, preserve identity scope and rollback. No mandatory large-model discovery/merger campaign, no MFM objective or release change.
